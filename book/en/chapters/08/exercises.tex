\section*{Exercises}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Exercises}

Pen and paper. The solutions are in \hyperref[ch:losningar]{appendix~A}.

\exercise{Decode}{Decoding}
\label{ex:8:1}
The following bytes go out on \code{MOSI}. For each one: read or write, which register, and which
value?
\begin{console}
a)  81 00 00 07 FF
b)  85 00 00 00 01
c)  8B 00 00 00 00
d)  C2 00 00 00 03
\end{console}
Two of them are unusual, each in its own way. Which, and why?

\exercise{Encode}{Calculation}
\label{ex:8:2}
Write out the complete five-byte transaction for each of the following:
\begin{enumerate}[a)]
  \item Read \code{RX_DLC}.
  \item Write \code{TX_DLC = 4}.
  \item Trigger a transmission.
  \item Acknowledge a received frame.
  \item Clear the error register.
\end{enumerate}
Note the difference between d) and e) in particular.

\exercise{What comes back}{Reasoning}
\label{ex:8:3}
\begin{enumerate}[a)]
  \item During a write the master clocks in five bytes on \code{MISO}. How many of them mean
    anything?
  \item During a read of \code{STATUS}, how many of the five mean anything, and which?
  \item A driver assembles the return value from \emph{all five} received bytes instead of the
    last four. What happens, and what would it look like while debugging?
\end{enumerate}

\exercise{\texorpdfstring{\code{SS}}{SS} and aborts}{Reasoning}
\label{ex:8:4}
What happens in each of the cases?
\begin{enumerate}[a)]
  \item \code{SS} goes high after three of five bytes in a write to \code{TX_ID}.
  \item The master holds \code{SS} low and sends ten bytes in a row:
    \code{81 00 00 01 23 82 00 00 00 02}.
  \item The master sends the command byte \code{0x91}.
\end{enumerate}
Case b) is the dangerous one. Why, and what would the symptom look like at bring-up?

\exercise{Time budget}{Calculation}
\label{ex:8:5}
At 1~MHz \code{SCK}:
\begin{enumerate}[a)]
  \item How long does a \code{receive()} that finds a frame take?
  \item How long does a maximal CAN frame on the bus take at 1~Mbit/s?
  \item Another node sends frames as fast as it can. What happens to the ones that arrive while
    your \code{receive()} is running? Is that your bug?
\end{enumerate}
